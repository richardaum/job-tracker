# ADR-001: Single-Phase Delivery of Per-User AI Key and AI Toggle

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-07-30

## Context

The app currently uses one shared OpenAI API key (server env var) to power 9 distinct AI-driven capabilities (job summary, auto-fill/extraction, AI chat, match analysis, note generation, company description, location inference, text rewrite/restructure). This centralizes cost and risk on the operator and gives users no control over whether AI runs on their data or account.

The product needs to move to a model where each user supplies and owns their OpenAI key, with a limited trial quota for new users before a key becomes required, plus an explicit per-user control to turn AI capabilities off regardless of key/quota state. During brainstorming, three delivery shapes were considered for how much of this ships together.

## Decision

Ship the full capability in a single phase (MVP): per-user OpenAI key management with save-time validation, usage-quota-based trial before a key is required, an independent manual AI on/off toggle, and click-time modal gating (redirecting to Settings) applied consistently across all AI entry points at launch.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Phased — key/quota first, flag/UI gating second

- **Description**: Phase 1 ships key configuration, validation, and trial-quota enforcement in the backend only (AI calls fail with a generic error once quota is exhausted and no key is set). Phase 2 adds the manual toggle and the consistent modal-based UI gating across all AI entry points.
- **Pros**: Smaller change surface per phase; validates the cost-control mechanism (the primary business driver) before touching UI across 9 call sites; lower regression risk per release.
- **Cons**: Ships an interim state where quota-exhausted users hit an unstyled/inconsistent error instead of the intended guided modal; requires two review/release cycles for one coherent user-facing feature.
- **Why rejected**: The user prioritized a single coherent release over incremental risk reduction, since the UI gating and backend enforcement are tightly coupled (the modal's trigger condition IS the backend quota/key/flag state) and shipping them apart would create a visibly unfinished experience in between.

### Alternative 2: Flag/UI first, key/quota second

- **Description**: Ship the manual AI on/off toggle and the modal-gated UI first (largely frontend, checking a single new boolean), deferring the BYOK key field, validation, and trial-quota mechanics to a later PRD.
- **Pros**: Fastest visible UX win; minimal backend risk in the first release.
- **Cons**: Does not address the actual driver for this change — moving AI cost/ownership off the shared env-var key — so the operator keeps paying for and being liable for all AI usage until the second phase ships.
- **Why rejected**: Defers the core problem (key ownership and cost control) behind a cosmetic toggle, inverting priority.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Users get one complete, consistent experience at launch: no intermediate state where some AI surfaces are gated and others are not.
- Cost/ownership shift (the main driver) and the UX control (the secondary ask) land together, avoiding a "half-migrated" perception across the 9 AI entry points.

### Negative

- Larger single change touches all 9 AI call sites plus the Settings tab and `UserSettingEntity` schema at once, increasing the size of the review and the blast radius of a single release.
- No intermediate checkpoint to validate the trial-quota mechanism in production before the full UX (toggle + modal) is also live.

### Risks

- Because backend enforcement (key/quota/flag state) and frontend gating (modal at 9 entry points) ship together, a defect in either can block all AI usage simultaneously for affected users. Mitigation: thorough test coverage per AI entry point before release, called out explicitly in the TechSpec.

## Implementation Notes

Defer to the TechSpec (`cy-create-techspec`) for how the single phase is broken into implementable tasks (e.g., schema migration, shared gating utility across the 9 call sites, key validation endpoint, modal component).
